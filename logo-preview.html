Rationale & Symbolism

The J's descender hooks right and rises back to become the M's left stem — a single gesture that forms both letters. The enclosed counter (the teardrop loop on the left) is the visual fingerprint of the mark. In penmanship and luxury type, enclosed loops signal confidence and refinement. The J and M share a stroke rather than merely sitting beside each other: they are genuinely one mark, not two letters.

The Inscription
Interlocking · Overlapping · Monogram tradition
Navigation
Footer
Jose Simon Muck
Rationale & Symbolism

The J's vertical is inscribed through the M's axis of symmetry — passing through the valley, extending above both stem tops and below the baseline. This is how engravers build classic overlapping monograms: one letter passes through the other, neither dominant alone. The J's hook exits quietly below the M's baseline, like a signature written underneath a document heading. The crown of the J above the M adds the final note of authority.

The Executive Pair
Formal · Slab terminals · Optically balanced
Navigation
Footer

Rationale & Symbolism

Two letters built to a shared system. The slab terminals on every stem end signal that this was constructed rather than typed — the same move used on the title pages of academic publications and legal documents. The optical weight differential (heavier stems, lighter inner diagonal) is borrowed from premium type design. The hairline baseline rule at the foot of both letters ties the pair into a single unit without enclosing them in a container.

The Superior Mark
Hierarchy · Author's attribution · Editorial
Navigation
Footer
Jose Simon Muck
Rationale & Symbolism

The J is positioned as a superior mark — smaller, higher, leading the eye into the M below. The hook of the J ends exactly where the M's left stem begins: the two letters share a single point, making this a connected mark rather than two separate glyphs. This arrangement mirrors how authors are attributed in editorial tradition: the given-name initial is the intimate mark, the surname initial is the public one. The J says "this is personal." The M says "this is mine."

The Oval Seal
Contained · Personal seal · Timeless

Rationale & Symbolism

The horizontal oval is the historic shape of the personal seal — not the circular stamp (too corporate) or the square (too institutional) but the oval: the form of a signet ring, a wax seal, a name plate on a pen or a leather notebook. The J and M sit divided by a hairline rule inside the ellipse, each letter occupying its half with breathing room. The oval contains and completes the mark — it says "this is the full name, not just an initial."
