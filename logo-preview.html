Three Concepts for the M Monogram

A refinement of the brand mark — same palette, same identity. The goal: a personal seal that reads as a signature, not a startup logo. Review before any global change.

Current mark — for reference
Chart / Mountain Abstraction

Strong as a product or startup mark. The intent to evolve: make it read as a personal brand — a name, a signature, a mark of authorship. The M concepts below replace this with a letterform rooted in José Muck's initial.

Signature Monogram

Jose Simon Muck
Design rationale

A single continuous path — the M drawn as if lifted from a signature. The inner V rises to two-thirds height rather than touching the baseline: a classical mark of refinement found in calligraphic and typographic tradition alike. Subtle cubic curves on the stems give the sense of a pen's natural momentum and weight. Small serif terminals at the feet anchor the letterform without making it feel historical.

Modern Calligraphic M

Jose Simon Muck
Design rationale

Classical calligraphic principle: downstrokes carry the weight, upstrokes are hairlines. The vertical stems communicate presence and decisiveness. The inner V retreats into near-invisibility — a ghost of a line — creating natural visual hierarchy within a single letterform. The slight lean of each stem (the calligraphic axis) signals that this was built from the tradition of the written mark, not the constructed shape. The most editorial of the three.

3
Executive Personal Seal
Contained · Authoritative · Timeless
Navigation
Footer
Jose Simon Muck
Design rationale

The circle is the oldest form of a personal seal — signet rings, wax stamps, notary marks. Containing the M creates a sense of authority and completeness. The M itself is clean and direct: no curves, no ornamentation. Its confidence comes from proportion and spacing alone. The faint inner glow ring adds material depth without decoration. This mark reads as a stamp of authorship — the most formal of the three.
